Introduction

Learning Language from 10M Tokens

A typical human child hears roughly 10 million words by age 2. However, large language models (LLMs) like GPT-4 are pretrained on trillions of tokens. The BabyLM Challenge asks: How can we build syntactically and semantically rich language representations with strict small-scale limitations? Our approach integrates syntactic constraints directly into the pretraining lifecycle.

Pretraining Architecture

Multi-Task Loss Orchestration

Under our parallel setup, the model weights are optimized via a unified combined loss function:
Total Loss = L_MLM + L_NSP + α * L_POS

During backpropagation, the gradients of the POS classifier propagate through the pooling matrix back to the base BERT encoder layers, adjusting representations to align simultaneously with semantics (MLM) and syntactic tag distributions.

Layered Pretraining

The Layered POS Vocabulary Filtering

Predicting 1-out-of-30,522 tokens is challenging for small scale models. However, grammar tells us that a masked slot has a single valid POS category. The **Layered POS** architecture first predicts POS tags at the token level, then retrieves an allowed vocabulary subset (e.g. noun words only) and overlays it onto the MLM softmax layer to reduce entropy.

How the Layered Constraints Reshape Logits

  • Narrowed search space: Filters out verbs, prepositions, or adjectives when predicting a noun slot.
  • Differentiable transitions (Soft Masking): Computes weighted combinations based on predictions to allow training backpropagation.
  • Syntactic constraints overlay: raw MLM logits are shifted by adding log(mask + epsilon).
